How the scores are worked out
  • Every day GDELT machine-reads news worldwide and codes each story's main event: who did what to whom, where.
  • Unrest is the share of that day's articles about fighting, bombings, assaults, mass violence or protests.
  • Crisis closeness is the share about one country threatening military force, posturing, fighting or committing mass violence against a different country.
  • Shares, not counts, so a quiet Sunday or a big sports day doesn't fake calm.
  • Each score is the 3-day average ranked against the previous 365 days. It says how today compares with the past year, not whether the world is safe.
  • Machine coding makes mistakes, such as ships counted under their flag country. The example stories let you check.
  • The script that builds the numbers is daily.py; the numbers themselves are in data/.
Data: The GDELT Project event files; Geopolitical Risk Index (Caldara and Iacoviello). Not advice of any kind.
